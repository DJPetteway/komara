# Komara Charter (L0)

## Purpose

Komara organizes intellectual and strategic capital to develop ideas, cultivate
leadership, and advance institutional reform across Africa and the global diaspora.

It sits between a think tank, an international leadership network, and a
reform-oriented institution. It does not try to deliver every service itself; it
connects people with the ideas, skills, money and relationships to the partner
organizations that do the work.

## The gap it addresses

Millions of people across the diaspora want to contribute to African nations and
to Haiti. No shared record shows what has been offered, to whom, and toward
which goal. Without that record, offers go unseen, effort is duplicated, and
trust is hard to build. Komara's first job is to keep that record.

## Five functions

1. **Develop ideas.** New approaches to governance, economic development and institutional reform.
2. **Cultivate leadership.** Connect thinkers and emerging leaders across the diaspora.
3. **Coordinate resources.** Match expertise, funding and partnerships to worthwhile initiatives.
4. **Influence institutions.** Research, policy proposals, diplomacy and collaboration.
5. **Build long-term capacity.** Reconstruction, education, infrastructure and economic
   self-determination, delivered through partner organizations.

## Principles

- **Sovereignty first.** Each nation sets its own priorities. Komara contributes; it does not dictate.
- **Independence.** Komara takes no orders from any party, government or foreign power. It draws on
  external agendas when they serve African and diasporic self-determination, and can set them aside.
- **One people, many places.** The continent and the diaspora (the Caribbean, the Americas, Europe)
  are one constituency. Haiti, the first Black republic, holds a central place in that history.
- **Accountability in public.** Every pledge goes on a record that anyone can verify.
- **Contribution over credentials.** People are judged by what they put forward and deliver.

## Lineage

Komara draws on Pan-African thought and practice: the Haitian Revolution, the
self-reliance tradition of Thomas Sankara, the sovereignty agenda now being
pursued in Burkina Faso and the wider Sahel, and the continental framework of
the African Union's *Agenda 2063: The Africa We Want*.

## Name

- **Komara Council**: the institution, meaning its strategic leadership and membership.
- **Komara Order**: the philosophy, set out in this charter.
- **Komara Project**: the long-term work, meaning the record and the initiatives it supports.

## Layers

- **L0 (this repository).** The charter, an agenda taxonomy, and a verifiable contribution record.
- **L1.** Contributor and initiative profiles, plus pledge-to-delivery tracking with partner sign-off.
- **L2.** Matching offers to needs, plus published reports by track and nation.
